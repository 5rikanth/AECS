MEDICAL EMERGENCY RESPONSE PROTOCOL (Reference Knowledge Base — academic prototype)

Primary service: Ambulance, in almost all cases. This category covers
breathing difficulty, chest pain, unconsciousness, seizures, severe
allergic reaction, poisoning, labour/pregnancy complications, diabetic
emergencies, stroke symptoms, and severe uncontrolled bleeding not tied to
an accident.

Secondary service: Police is rarely needed unless the caller specifically
mentions a crime connection (e.g. assault causing the medical emergency) —
in that case use "multiple".

Severity indicators:
- CRITICAL: unconsciousness, not breathing or severe breathing difficulty,
  seizure in progress, stroke symptoms (one-sided weakness/numbness),
  suspected poisoning, heavy bleeding, known heart patient in acute
  distress.
- HIGH: active labour pain, asthma attack, severe allergic reaction,
  diabetic emergency with low sugar, persistent severe pain.
- MEDIUM: high fever not responding to home care, vomiting/dizziness,
  general weakness without a named acute symptom above.
- LOW: mild/manageable symptoms explicitly described as not severe.
- UNKNOWN: caller mentions "medical help needed" with no symptom detail.

Notes: Breathing difficulty ("saans lene mein takleef/problem") on its own,
even without other detail, should be treated as at least HIGH severity —
respiratory distress is one of the least safe symptoms to under-triage.
